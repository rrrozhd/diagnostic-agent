A research assistant for doctors: suggests possibilities, shows where every claim comes from, flags what must not be missed.

The copilot suggests, cites and flags.
The doctor judges, orders tests and prescribes.

The problem

  • A doctor has minutes to weigh possible conditions, the tests that separate them, and treatments safe for this patient.
  • Nobody can keep up with the thousands of papers published every day.
  • Rare but dangerous conditions get buried under "most likely".
  • Today's AI chatbots sound sure and can invent sources.

Guardrails

  • No source, no claim, and the source must actually say it. Withdrawn papers are never shown.
  • Looks for evidence against each possibility, not only for it.
  • Must-not-miss conditions are always shown, from a doctor-curated list, not AI judgement.
  • Drug warnings come from reference databases; the AI can explain one but not switch it off.
  • The doctor decides: treatment research waits for their pick.
  • Honest wording, missing information listed, names removed before any outside search.

Next steps

  • Recruit doctor reviewers; sign privacy agreements before real data.
